# Getting unstuck

A stage or task is stuck when its original done-check is unmet and nothing underway can meet it. Read the task, applicable process, triggering input, and requested outcome, then follow this flow.

1. **Recover saved progress first.** Check saved progress, run history, completed outputs, and accepted findings before launching or repeating work. Separate the work outcome from executor, session, or return-route availability—do not redo completed work solely because a callback or session is unavailable. Preserve explicit named executor assignments and established authentication; incidental relay or session ancestry is not an extra completion requirement.

2. **Decide what can proceed.** Classify the current state before acting:
   - **Ready:** execute the next step.
   - **Underway:** a healthy running task or an event-owned wait with a responsible owner, checkpoint, and supported return route is active—not stuck and not complete. Elapsed waiting alone is not failure; retain the checkpoint and resumption ownership.
   - **Completion claimed:** verify against the original done-check (step 8).
   - **Blocked:** an unmet check with nothing underway, an unsupported wake or return route, or a wait lacking an owner or checkpoint requires diagnosis. Change ineffective coordination rather than repeating waits solely to hold a descendant alive.

3. **Locate the unmet need and trace the block.** State in business terms what each unmet part of the done-check requires. Trace each blocking condition to its source—owner direction, process contract, required outcome, record, or assessment—when it was produced, and whether its basis still holds. Reuse valid evidence and identify the relevant causal change before reopening an accepted conclusion. Refresh conditions resting on stale evidence or changed rules, and correct conditions that exceed the requested outcome rather than treating them as binding. Preserve the requested outcome; never relabel scores or skip required checks.

4. **Diagnose the cause before correcting.** Classify what actually failed: observed product failure; execution, environment, permission, or delivery failure; assessment setup error; or an unsupported or stale blocking condition. Test the cheapest supported hypothesis first rather than working through incident-specific assumptions; a single explanation is a guess until evidence supports it.

5. **Make the smallest authorized causal correction.** Complete necessary dependency repair within existing authority, including M3NT0R service, prompt, or instruction repair when that is the demonstrated dependency. This does not authorize weakening the owner's outcome or acceptance to pass, or changing authentication, accounts, providers, or billing. Preserve valid evidence and existing customer, executor, and scope boundaries. Work normally associated with a later stage may serve the present done-check; run or queue it under the current stage and name the need it serves.

6. **Retry only affected work after a causal change.** Once a relevant correction is in place, retry only the affected work and its necessary dependencies, retaining the original failing input and original acceptance criteria. When an attempt does not move the condition, feed what was learned back into diagnosis and change the approach. Stop demonstrated no-progress spending immediately while preserving saved results, checkpoints, and recovery ownership. No fixed attempt count triggers an owner question.

7. **Escalate owner-held blockers only.** Resolve conflicting instructions by the applicable owner direction first rather than asking the owner merely because two instructions differ. Escalate a specific missing owner-held authority, credential, business decision, or genuine scope expansion—with what you learned and your recommendation—and continue any independent authorized work.

8. **Verify, save, and deliver the outcome.** Verify against the original done-check using existing flow, eval, or assessment conventions—do not prescribe a new framework or a universal requirement for real customer work, keep synthetic setup proof separate from actual customer or operational evidence, and remove temporary demonstration artifacts after recording results. Put outcome evidence where the existing process or check reads it (process record, documentation home, plate, or stage assessment), save the result and history, and deliver it through a supported return route. A diagnosis, queued handoff, healthy wait, synthetic check, or dependency success does not complete an unmet parent outcome; retain the parent's next owned action or event until that outcome is verified and consumed.
