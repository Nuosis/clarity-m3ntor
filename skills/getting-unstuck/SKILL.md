# Getting unstuck

A stage is stuck when its done-check is unmet and nothing under way can meet it. Getting it unstuck is the work. Waiting is never the plan.

1. **Find the need.** For each part of the done-check still unmet, say in business terms what it needs to see: evidence that a flow is followed, a source read this week, a routine that ran on its own.

2. **Check where each blocking condition comes from.** Name the rule, record, assessment or answer that imposes it, when it was produced, and whether its basis still holds. A condition that rests on out-of-date evidence or on a rule that has since changed gets refreshed (re-read, re-run, re-assessed), not waited on.

3. **List the assumptions that keep the block in place** ("only real customer work can show this", "this rule cannot change", "that belongs to a later stage") and test the cheapest first. A single explanation is a guess until evidence supports it.

4. **Meet the need where you can.** Build what produces the missing evidence in the business's own systems: a routine, a record, a document, a proof of concept, sample data, an eval or a true-to-life scenario. Run it, show the result, then remove what you created for the demonstration. Work a later stage would normally do is allowed when this need requires it; queue it under the current stage and name the need it serves.

5. **Name double binds.** When two rules (stage work, owner direction, goal outcome, assessment criteria) cannot both be met, ask the owner to adjust one, with your recommendation, and carry on with whatever does not depend on the answer.

6. **Show it working.** Once new evidence exists, put it where the check reads it: the process record, the documentation home, the plate, or a stage assessment task, so M3NT0R sees it.

7. **Take the smallest action that changes a blocking condition.** Never change the M3NT0R service, its prompts, its stage rules or your own instructions to make a check pass or a review stop. Never relabel a score or skip a check.

8. **Learn from each attempt.** When an action does not move the condition, say why and test a different assumption. After two attempts on the same condition, ask the owner, with what you learned and your recommendation.
